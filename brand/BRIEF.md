# Brand brief: Crested Crane (Uganda)

Slug and repo: `uganda`. Country: Uganda (UG). Brand type: country. Brief v1, 2026-10-26. Facts below were checked on that date. Anything marked *verify* needs an official source before launch.

## 1. Name

**Crested Crane** (assigned, kept). The grey crowned crane, which Ugandans call the crested crane, is the national bird. It stands on the flag inside the white disc and on the coat of arms (*verify wording*). It feeds at the edge of swamps and grassland, where papyrus meets pasture. The name is plain English, two short words with a soft repeat, and anyone who has looked at Uganda's flag has seen the bird. It also carries the brand idea: Uganda lives at its edges (forest and savannah, river and swamp), and that is where we plan.
- Search check: two web searches (2026-10-26) found no tour operator trading as Crested Crane. The near misses were **Crested Crane Bwindi**, a lodge in the Buhoma sector, **Crowned Crane Safaris**, a small Uganda operator with a branch in Kisoro, and **Four Crane Safaris** in Kampala. None is the same name in the same trade, so the name stays, but the lodge is a real confusion risk (see risks).
- Trademark: a human must search the **Uganda Registration Services Bureau** (URSB, https://ursb.go.ug, classes 39 and 43), plus **UKIPO**, **EUIPO** and **USPTO**. The domain and social handles are also a human task, and the owner may want to tell Crested Crane Bwindi before launch.
- Risks: the lodge in Bwindi; other Ugandan businesses may use the bird's name, as it is a national symbol. Use of the flag or coat of arms in the logo is not allowed: the wordmark must not reproduce them.

Runners-up:
1. **Papyrus**: the swamp reed of Mabamba and the Nile. Quiet and specific, but Papyrus is a well-known font, which hurts search.
2. **Shoebill**: the bird every serious birder comes for. Memorable, but narrows the brand to birding.
3. **Kazinga**: the channel between Lakes George and Edward, famous for its boat trip. Easy to say, but less known than the others outside East Africa.

## 2. Positioning

*For travellers who want primates, birds and big game in one wild country: Uganda planned by forest sector, river and swamp, with the long drives cut short or flown.*

The gap. Uganda's premium supply is thin and lodge-led: Volcanoes Safaris (its own lodges at Bwindi and Kyambura), Wildplaces (Clouds Mountain Gorilla Lodge, Ishasha) and Sanctuary Gorilla Forest Camp. UK specialists mostly sell Uganda as "cheaper gorillas than Rwanda" or a Rwanda add-on, and local operators sell long minibus circuits. Nobody owns a **design-literate, single-country** edit: the right Bwindi sector for the traveller, chimps in Kibale, the Nile at Murchison, Kidepo by air, and serious birding, paced with scheduled flights so the trip isn't spent on the road.

## 3. Audience

**A. The primate-and-safari couple (UK/US, 45–65, 12–16 nights).**
- Motivation: gorillas and chimps, then savannah and the Nile. Wants comfort and good guides, and to see more than Rwanda's three parks in one trip.
- Objections: long drives, security near the DRC border, Ebola news, and lodge standards.

**B. The serious birder or naturalist (UK/Netherlands/US, 50s–70s, 14–18 nights).**
- Motivation: shoebill, Albertine Rift endemics, green-breasted pitta, a long list. Wants a bird guide who is truly expert.
- Objections: needs to know the specific sites and months. Doesn't want a generic safari with birds as an extra.

## 5. Voice

1. **Field-sharp.** Name the species, the sector, the month. Naturalist precision.
   - Do: "Ruhija, for the Albertine Rift endemics. Go in June."
   - Don't: "Spot amazing exotic birds in the jungle!"
2. **Plain about the road.** Say how long, how bumpy, and when to fly instead.
   - Do: "Kampala to Bwindi is a full day by road. We'd fly to Kihihi."
   - Don't: "Enjoy a scenic drive through the countryside."
3. **Warm, without the brochure.** Uganda is friendly; show it through people with names and jobs, not slogans.
   - Do: "Your Kibale guide has walked with this chimp community for years. Ask her about the alpha."
   - Don't: "Experience the warm smiles of the Pearl of Africa!"
- Banned: Pearl of Africa, exotic, hidden gem, untouched, primitive, tribe, Big Five (Uganda's only rhinos are at the Ziwa sanctuary), breathtaking, unforgettable, bucket list, once in a lifetime, "jungle" for montane forest.

Sample headlines:
- Four sectors of Bwindi. One of them is right for you.
- Chimps first. Gorillas second. The Nile last.
- A shoebill before breakfast.
- Fly the long legs. Walk the rest.
- Kidepo: the valley most visitors never reach.

## 9. Licensing route (human decision)

- **In Uganda:** tour operators must register with and be licensed by the **Uganda Tourism Board** under the Tourism Act 2008 (https://utb.go.ug). UWA permits are booked by registered operators. Membership of the Association of Uganda Tour Operators (https://auto.or.ug) is voluntary but common. The practical route is to contract a UTB-licensed Ugandan operator and record its licence.
- **Selling to UK customers through a UK company:** ATOL for flight-inclusive packages (https://www.caa.co.uk/atol-protection/) plus insolvency protection under the Package Travel Regulations 2018.
- **US:** check seller-of-travel registration for California, Florida, Washington and Hawaii.

## 10. Top 3 risks

1. **The DRC border, security and Ebola.** FCDO has no advise-against areas for Uganda (checked 2026-10-26), but Bwindi, Mgahinga, Ishasha, Semuliki and the Rwenzoris all sit near the DRC border, where the 2023 attacks happened and where the border is now closed for Ebola. Every booking in the west needs a fresh FCDO and US advice check, ranger escorts where required, and a written fallback. Do not sell Semuliki at launch. Check Kidepo (Karamoja) against the US advisory.
2. **LGBT+ safety.** The Anti-Homosexuality Act 2023 affects some travellers directly. The FAQ must give honest, sourced advice and link to FCDO, without scaring or dismissing.
3. **The name.** Crested Crane Bwindi (a lodge in our core destination) and Crowned Crane Safaris create confusion; URSB and UKIPO are unchecked, and the bird is a national symbol. Never use the flag or coat of arms in branding.
